Matrix Games Between Full Siblings in Mendelian Populations

Authors: József Garay, Tamás Varga, Villő Csiszár, Tamás F. Móri, and András Szilágyi

Journal: PLOS ONE (2025) · DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0331044

Core Objective: Classical Evolutionary Game Theory assumes individuals meet strangers at random. In nature, however, siblings within family broods or nests interact directly for resources. Because siblings share alleles through Mendelian inheritance, this paper demonstrates how sexual reproduction alters evolutionary game outcomes (e.g. donation games and prisoner's dilemmas), determining when altruism and cooperation can invade, stabilize, or achieve an Evolutionarily Stable Genotype Distribution (ESGD).
1

Mendelian Inheritance

Organisms inherit one allele from each parent ($a$ or $A$), forming genotypes $aa$, $aA$, or $AA$. Punnett squares define the segregation probabilities for each parental cross.

2

Sibling Game Interactions

Siblings within a family play a 2-player matrix game. Each genotype determines whether an individual Cooperates (C) or Defects (D). Payoffs directly dictate survival.

3

Evolution Across Generations

Families with higher surviving offspring contribute more alleles to the gene pool. Continuous differential equations track genotype frequencies over time to equilibrium.

📐 How the Mathematics Works (Expand to view equations) ▼
1. Mendelian Offspring Probabilities
$$p_k(ij) = \sum_{\alpha \in \text{gametes}(i)} \sum_{\beta \in \text{gametes}(j)} P(\alpha) P(\beta) \cdot \mathbb{I}(\alpha \cup \beta = k)$$

Each parent contributes one allele at random (50%). Offspring probability is the product of independent parental alleles.

2. Family Genotype Survival Equation (Table 1)
$$n_k(ij) = n \cdot p_k(ij) \sum_{l=1}^3 \left[ p_l(ij) \cdot \mathbf{s}_k^T \mathbf{A} \mathbf{s}_l \right]$$

An offspring of genotype $k$ meets a sibling of genotype $l$ with probability $p_l(ij)$. Game payoff $\mathbf{s}_k^T \mathbf{A} \mathbf{s}_l$ dictates survival.

3. Genotype Production Rate
$$V_k(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{N}{2} \sum_{i=1}^3 \sum_{j=1}^3 x_i x_j n_k(ij)$$

Summed across all 9 random parental crosses in the population weighted by mating frequencies $x_i x_j$.

4. Population Dynamics Differential Equation (Eq. 1)
$$\frac{dx_k}{dt} = V_k(\mathbf{x}) - x_k \sum_{j=1}^3 V_j(\mathbf{x})$$

Continuous-time ODE tracking frequency changes while strictly preserving normalization $\sum x_k = 1$.

Recessive Mode

aa Fixation

Final: aa → 99.98%, aA → 0%, AA → 0%

Pure cooperator families ($aa \times aa$) produce 100% cooperating offspring who mutually reward each other, driving allele $a$ to homozygous fixation.

Dominant Mode

Coexistence

Final: aa ≈ 80.6%, aA ≈ 18.4%, AA ≈ 1.0%

Because both $aa$ and $aA$ cooperate, defection alleles hide in heterozygous individuals without being eliminated, maintaining a stable polymorphic coexistence.

Intermediate Mode

Near Fixation

Final: aa ≈ 98.8%, aA ≈ 1.2%, AA → 0%

Heterozygotes use a mixed 50/50 strategy. The selective advantage of pure cooperator pairings strongly drives the population toward allele $a$.
